<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><i>If you've ever had </i><b style="font-style: italic;">Faceboo</b>k<b style="font-style: italic;"> ba</b><i>n your account without warning, you know the feeling. This is the story of how my 22-year-old account disappeared on Christmas Eve. I built a step-by-step playbook to fight back.</i></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Picture this. It's Christmas Eve, and instead of a warm mug of cocoa, you get a piping hot notice that twenty-two years of your digital life just went:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:quote -->
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><!-- wp:jetpack/markdown {"source":"**\u0022Poof. Gone.\u0022**"} -->
<div class="wp-block-jetpack-markdown"><p><strong>“Poof. Gone.”</strong></p>

<!-- /wp:quote -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Like the last donut at a morning meeting. That's what happened to me. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Other people were opening presents or pretending to like their in-laws. Meanwhile, I was staring at a screen. It informed me that my Facebook account had been permanently disabled.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>No warning. No explanation. No context. Just a message as cheerful as a tax audit. Facebook ban. I was Zucked.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h5 id="the-digital-death-sentence" class="wp-block-heading has-text-color has-background has-link-color" style="color:#c2f104;background-color:#06297a">Two Decades of Social Media Evolution. THEN FACEBOOK BAN.</h5>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This wasn't just any account. This was my <em>award-winning</em> Facebook account. "<strong>Biggest Social Media Oversharer</strong>" at my ten-year high school reunion, thank you very much. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2165,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A meticulously curated archive contained memories, milestones, grief, and joy. It included memes, sobriety anniversaries, and family losses. There were inside jokes and way too many opinions about reality TV shows that probably didn’t deserve the emotional investment. And it was gone in an instant. The timing felt particularly cruel. Christmas Eve: a day when most customer service departments are as reachable as Santa's workshop.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It's the digital equivalent of being kicked off the party bus. Except that, the party bus holds all your family photos. It serves as your primary means of staying connected to distant relatives.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Did they have no sense of legacy? I'd been using Facebook since 2004, back then you needed a ".edu" email to sign up. I'd watched it evolve from a simple college networking site to the digital town square where democracy goes to die</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>I dropped Myspace Tom faster than a three day old "Top 8 Friends" list.</p>

<h6 id="into-the-appeal-Abyss" class="wp-block-heading has-text-color has-background has-link-color" style="color:#c2f104;background-color:#06297a">Understanding Meta’s Appeal Process: Challenges and Comparisons to a Roomba</h6>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Most people would have called it quits and said oh well. Why?</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.oversightboard.com/transparency-reports/" type="link" id="https://www.theverge.com/2021/10/25/22745242/facebook-oversight-board-appeals-transparency-report">Because as I was about to discover, Meta's appeal process is like arguing with a Roomba. It bumps into a wall, spins in a circle, and pretends not to notice you. Only about twenty-four percent of appeals go anywhere. Paying for Meta Verified support mostly gets you a shinier automated brush-off from agents named Solange.</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It was confusing hedge maze with has more grey areas than my Aunt Linda's mystery casserole at Thanksgiving. I was down the rabbit hole. Things were getting curiouser and curiouser.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:quote -->
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"elements":{"link":{"color":{"text":"#ffe23d"}}},"color":{"text":"#ffe23d"},"typography":{"textAlign":"center"}},"backgroundColor":"vivid-red","fontSize":"x-large"} -->
<p class="has-text-align-center has-vivid-red-background-color has-text-color has-background has-link-color has-x-large-font-size" style="color:#ffe23d"><strong>"Caleb in Metaland"</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></blockquote>
<!-- /wp:quote -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You know me, I'm gay and stubborn. I also love tea parties. And this Facebook ban was giving me life!!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2180,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://indiedocjourney.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/download-890x1024.png" alt="A colorful cartoon scene depicting a whimsical version of 'Meta.land' with caricatures of characters including a man in a dress with a rainbow sash, a blue mad hatter, and a muscular man in bunny ears. <!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>So I said nope to giving up and pilled a full Leslie Knope instead.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The appeal process itself deserves its own anthropological study. First, you're presented with a form that asks you to explain what you did wrong. It already assumes guilt. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The drop down menus for violation types don't include "Bitch, I didn't do shit." </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You're forced to guess at your own crime like some dystopian game show. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Then comes the waiting. Days turn into weeks. Weeks turn into months. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2182,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://indiedocjourney.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/didntdoshit-1024x559.png" alt="Screenshot of a Facebook appeal form displaying options for violations with an aggressive statement highlighted." class="wp-image-2182"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The automated responses arrive with the regularity of a Swiss watch. They offer the helpfulness of a belt and suspenders but no pants. Each one essentially says, "We've reviewed your appeal and determined that you're still guilty of something we won't specify."</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The most infuriating part was the gas-lighting. The responses would reference "community standards" violations without citing which standards or what content. It was like being told you failed a test but not being allowed to see the questions or your answers. The platform seemed designed not to resolve issues but to exhaust you into acceptance.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":5,"style":{"color":{"text":"#c2f104","background":"#06297a"},"elements":{"link":{"color":{"text":"#c2f104"}}}},"anchor":"detective-work-finding-the-smoking-gun"} -->
<h5 id="detective-work-finding-the-smoking-gun" class="wp-block-heading has-text-color has-background has-link-color" style="color:#c2f104;background-color:#06297a"> Accidental Cyber Detective: Uncovering Online Mysteries</h5>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I documented the whole experience. Screenshots. Timestamps. Notes. Emails. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I'm a documentary filmmaker, so I treated it like a job. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p> I even started paying Meta for Meta Verified, because it comes with the promise of elevated HUMAN support. Capital letters doing a lot of work there. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What I got instead was a maze of catch-22s, broken promises, and flat-out falsehoods.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2184,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://indiedocjourney.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/banged-584x1024.png" alt="An illustrated office scene depicting multiple customer service representatives wearing Meta-branded shirts, sitting at desks with computers. <!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p> In the beginning, they tried to shame me. They literally told me my account was disabled because I was a bad person. When I asked what I had done to merit account closure, they responded with, "You know what you did." Which I haven't heard out loud since I was a toddler. It was dismissive. Infantilizing. It made me feel like a bad dog who'd chewed up the furniture but couldn't remember doing it. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":6,"style":{"color":{"text":"#c2f104","background":"#06297a"},"elements":{"link":{"color":{"text":"#c2f104"}}}}} -->
<h6 class="wp-block-heading has-text-color has-background has-link-color" style="color:#c2f104;background-color:#06297a">The cookie monsterS: A ROad to Facebook ban perdition</h6>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That's when I realized I needed proof. The breakthrough came almost by accident. While desperately trying every possible avenue to regain access, I stumbled upon<a href="https://www.facebook.com/hacked?src=helpcenter_hackedindex"> hacked.facebook.com</a>—a link I'd never heard of but decided to try in my increasingly frantic attempts to understand what had happened.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I entered my password and was redirected to a link that looked… odd. I looked closer and did a digital spit-take. It read: </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:pullquote {"style":{"elements":{"link":{"color":{"text":"var:preset|color|primary"}}}},"backgroundColor":"vivid-red","textColor":"primary","fontSize":"medium"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-pullquote has-primary-color has-vivid-red-background-color has-text-color has-background has-link-color has-medium-font-size"><blockquote><p><em><a href="http://www.facebook.com/checkpoint/disabled/login_with_hacked_cookie" type="link" id="http://www.facebook.com/checkpoint/disabled/login_with_hacked_cookie"><strong>www.facebook.com/checkpoint/disabled/login_with_hacked_cookie</strong></a></em></p></blockquote></figure>
<!-- /wp:pullquote -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p> That phrase isn't decorative. "Login with hacked cookie" is Facebook's own internal language.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2186,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://indiedocjourney.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/image-572x1024.png" alt="Infographic explaining cookie theft and its impact on Facebook accounts, illustrating the login process, the method of cookie theft, consequences, and the importance of data security." class="wp-image-2186"/></figure>

<!-- wp:quote -->
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><!-- wp:group {"layout":{"type":"flex","flexWrap":"nowrap"}} -->
<div class="wp-block-group"><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://owasp.org/www-community/attacks/Session_hijacking_attack" type="link" id="https://owasp.org/www-community/attacks/Session_hijacking_attack"><strong>Cookie theft</strong>: A breach where a hacker doesn't need your password because it doesn't trigger two-factor authentication.</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>
<!-- /wp:group --></blockquote>
<!-- /wp:quote -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It's the digital equivalent of someone stealing your house keys and then acting surprised when you notice the furniture moved. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This was my smoking gun. Facebook's own system informed me that my account had been compromised through cookie theft. This is a sophisticated attack. As the account holder, I would have no way to prevent or even detect it until it was too late.</p>
